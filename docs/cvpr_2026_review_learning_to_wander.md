## Paper Summary

Summarize in your own words the paper, its key ideas, its contributions and their significance. This summary will help the AC and the authors understand the rest of your review and be confident that you understand the paper.

## Paper Summary Response

This paper addresses the limitations of static image geolocation by introducing an interactive, embodied approach. The authors present WanderBench, a global benchmark consisting of 32,741 panoramas organized into navigable graphs across six continents. Unlike previous datasets that rely on discrete, static images, WanderBench allows agents to perform actions like rotation and movement to gather visual evidence. The benchmark's locations and graphs are constructed via an LMM-in-the-loop process, which matters for how results should be interpreted.

The authors also propose GeoAoT, a framework that enables Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to reduce geographic uncertainty by generating actionable plans during inference, such as approaching landmarks or adjusting viewpoints. Evaluation is performed bidirectionally, measuring both the model's ability to solve geolocation tasks and its ability to propose diverse, difficulty-calibrated locations. Experiments on ~20 LMMs suggest that active exploration often improves fine-grained localization accuracy, under the paper's evaluation protocol.

## Paper Strengths Response

- **Shift in Paradigm:** The move from static image classification/retrieval to an embodied, "active exploration" framework is a logical evolution for the field. It better mimics how human experts solve geolocation tasks (e.g., in GeoGuessr).
- **Dataset Utility:** WanderBench could be useful as a globally scoped dataset with navigation support, assuming the release format and licensing make it practically usable to the community.. Most existing large-scale datasets are either local or lack the graph structure needed for agent-based research.
- **Model Agnostic Framework:** GeoAoT operates at inference time without requiring additional training, making it easily applicable to various existing and future LMMs.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The inclusion of "location proposing" as a metric provides a new way
  to assess how models generate plausible locations under difficulty settings. The validity of tis depends on how the proposing task is defined and controlled (e.g., avoiding famous-place bias).

## Major Weaknesses Response

- **Circular Evaluation Bias:** The paper states that all benchmark locations are generated by LMMs.
  - Testing LMMs on a dataset created by other LMMs introduces significant risk of "model-based bias." The dataset may inadvertently favor locations or features that LMMs are already biased toward, may not reflect to the true complexity of real-world "in-the-wild" images that a human might take.
  - They also generate the questions with LMMs, which adds another source of model-shaped bias.
  - While using LMM's for data generation allows for impressive scale like the 32K here, it introduces a potential self-reinforcing loop where the models are tested on exactly the types of features they are biased to generate.
- **Data provenance / licensing / reproducibility:** The paper does not clearly specify which streetview providers are used, what exactly will be released (raw imagery vs URLs/IDs vs features), and whether redistribution is permitted. For a benchmark paper, the release plan needs to be explicit and verifiable.
- **Training-data contamination risk:** Many evaluated LMMs may have seen similar streetview or map imagery during training. The paper does not provide any contamination/near-duplicate analysis or any region-based holdout construction, so it is hard to separate generalization from memorization.
  **Baseline fairness / attribution:** GeoAoT gets multiple observations through navigation. Comparing against single-shot "Direct Answering" (as in Fig 4) is not enough to attribute gains to the AoT prompting rather than "more views." At minimum, compare to baselines with the same evidence budget: (i) a scripted policy (e.g., rotate N times), (ii) a random walk, and (iii) a non-reflective multi-view baseline that consumes the same set of views GeoAoT saw but without the AoT reflection step.
- **Computational Latency and Cost:** The paper does not provide an analysis of the overhead introduced by the iterative AoT process. Since LMM inference is expensive and slow, the "cost-per-accuracy-gain" is important practical consideration that is not quantified.
- **Environment Interaction Clarity:** The paper mentions that the agent executes actions like "Move forward 10 meters". However, the dataset is composed of discrete panorama nodes. It is unclear how the model handles cases where a proposed action (e.g., "rotate 30 degrees") does not align perfectly with the available nodes or pre-rendered views in the navigation graph.
  **Key protocol details are underspecified:** It is unclear how "street/city/country" correctness is computed from GPS (radius thresholds? reverse geocoding service?), and how "difficulty/coverage (20/50/80)" is assigned. The "location proposing" task also needs clearer validity criteria and controls against trivial strategies (e.g., repeatedly proposing famous places).
- **Novelty framing is not fully convincing:** The interactive street-view graph setup overlaps with prior navigation environments and tool/agent loops. The paper should be more precise about what is new here (e.g., scale, global coverage, bidirectional proposing, or specific evaluation protocol) and make any "first" claims specific. the novelty claims would be clearer with sharper positioning against prior navigation/tool-agent setups

## Justification Response

The paper proposes a relevant and interesting shift toward embodied geolocation, and the WanderBench dataset is a substantial contribution in terms of scale and structure. However, the methodology of using LMMs to generate the benchmark raises serious concerns about evaluation validity and circularity. Furthermore, the performance gains of the GeoAoT framework are not adequately decoupled from the simple benefit of the model seeing more images.

**Suggestions for Rebuttal:**

- **Release/provenance clarity:** Specify providers, what is released (images vs IDs/URLs vs features), and confirm that licensing/terms allow the intended release and reuse.
- **Address Evaluation Bias:** Provide evidence that the LMM-generated locations are representative of real-world diversity and do not just reflect the internal biases of the generator models. Comparing a subset of WanderBench against a human-curated dataset (like Im2GPS) would be helpful.
- **Stronger baselines (same evidence budget):** Compare GeoAoT to baselines that see the same views but do not do AoT style reflection (e.g., scripted rotate, random walk, or sequential multi-view prompting without reflection). If possible, also test giving the baseline the exact set of views GeoAoT visited, in some reasonable formatting, to isolate whether the gains come from the prompting strategy vs additional evidence.
- **Efficiency Analysis:** Provide a table showing the avg number of turns, time-to-completion, and estimated API costs for GeoAoT compared to single-shot baselines.
- **Action Space Implementation:** Clarify how discrete panorama nodes handle continuous action requests (e.g., rotating a specific number of degrees).
- **Contamination check:** Provide a minimal near-duplicate/overlap analysis or a region-based holdout construction to reduce the risk that results reflect memorization.

The framing of the work can use refinement: The authors mention "static methods" having a drawback that they fail to include exploration that humans might employ, but why is that bad?

The baseline set for non-LMM geolocation is limited to GeoCLIP. Please justify this choice and clarify whether other strong baselines were considered but excluded due to licensing, code availability, or input modality mismatch.

Why do they compare to one static method (GeoCLIP), but not other SOTA ones like PIGEON/PIGEOTTO (Haas et. al.)? What is the reasoning behind chosing to compare with GeoCLIP but not other methods? Without a comparison to specialized models like PIGEOTTO, it is difficult to assess if GeoAoT's performance is competitive with the current state-of-the-art in this domain.
